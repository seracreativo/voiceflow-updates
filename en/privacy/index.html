What gets stored, and where

No legalese and no hedging. If something here doesn't sit right with you, better to know before you install it than after.

01 · The important bit

The audio and the text don't leave your Mac.

The transcription is done by the system's own speech engine, and the cleanup is handled by rules and the language model macOS already ships with. There is no server of ours in the middle.

This isn't a privacy policy: the code that would send the audio anywhere doesn't exist. You can check it without taking our word for it — turn off the Wi-Fi and keep dictating.

  • The audio isn't stored anywhere, not even on your Mac: it's transcribed and discarded.
  • The dictated text stays in your local history.
  • There are no accounts, no sign-up, no email.

02 · What gets stored

All on your Mac, and not much of it.

It lives in the app's preferences file, next to the settings. There's no database and no hidden folder.

  • The history — the last 200 transcriptions. At that point the oldest one falls off.
  • Your vocabulary — the terms you teach it.
  • The settings — the shortcut, the language, the level for each app.
  • The licence, in your iCloud Keychain.
  • The Whisper models, if you turn Whisper on, in Application Support.

03 · What travels

Nineteen settings, and not the history.

If you have iCloud, your settings sync across your devices. The list is explicit in the code and contains settings only: the shortcut, the language, the tone, the vocabulary and the translator's preferences.

The history isn't on that list, and that's not an oversight. It's the most sensitive thing the app holds — everything you've dictated — and taking it off the device is the decision the rest of the program refuses to make on its own.

The list is closed on purpose: with a «sync whatever is in preferences», the day someone added a key without thinking, the history would start travelling, and nobody finds that out until it's late.

04 · The permissions

Two of them, and the ones it doesn't ask for.

macOS will ask for the microphone, so it can hear you, and Accessibility, which is the only way the system lets an app type inside another one. Without the second, VoiceFlow can transcribe but it can't type for you.

Just as important is what it does not ask for, because that's where other dictation apps take liberties:

  • Screen recording — it doesn't ask, and it won't
  • Camera, contacts, calendar, photos, location — none of them
  • Access to your folders — not Desktop, not Documents, not Downloads

05 · The connections

None of them carries the audio or the dictated text.

06 · The exception

Polishing a translation with a large model.

There is one feature, off out of the box, that sends the already translated text to a Google model so it reads more naturally. It handles idioms the on-device model won't touch.

That does leave your Mac, and it's the opposite of what the rest of the app promises. That's why it ships disabled, why the access key is yours and not ours, and why the setting says so in these same words instead of talking about «the cloud».

It doesn't affect dictation. If you never turn it on, it never happens.

07 · Deleting it

All of it, in a moment.

The history is cleared from inside the app, all at once or entry by entry. The settings and the history live in the preferences file for com.seracreativo.voiceflow; deleting the app and that file leaves nothing behind.

The licence is in your iCloud Keychain and is removed from Settings › Licence. Because the keychain syncs, it goes from all your devices at once.
