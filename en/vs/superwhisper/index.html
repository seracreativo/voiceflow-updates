VoiceFlow or Superwhisper

The usual argument doesn't work here. Superwhisper also runs offline, also sends your voice to no server, and can also be paid for once. The difference is somewhere else.

01 · Common ground

On what matters, they agree with us.

Both apps think the same about the things that actually count, and that goes first, before looking for differences.

  • Both work with no internet.
  • Neither sends your voice to a server.
  • Both can be paid for once, with no subscription.
  • And they cover more languages and more platforms: 100+ against our 30, and Windows on top of Mac and iPhone.

02 · The difference

One downloads a model; the other doesn't.

Superwhisper uses Whisper, OpenAI's model, and to dictate you have to fetch one: between 60 MB and over a gigabyte depending on which, and you have to choose.

VoiceFlow uses the recognition engine macOS 26 already ships. That's why the download is 6.5 MB and it dictates from the first minute: what it needs came with the system.

The first time you open a dictation app you want to dictate, not compare models. That's the whole difference.

03 · And yet

VoiceFlow can use Whisper too.

It's in the settings, switched off. Anyone who wants it turns it on and downloads the same model they'd use in any other app: accuracy goes up and so does the weight, exactly the same.

We say so because it changes what this comparison means. It isn't «our engine against theirs». It's that with ours you don't have to fetch anything, and if one day you need to, it's there.
